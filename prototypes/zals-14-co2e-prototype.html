Q7 · Landfill factor

  
IPCC 2019 Refinement, Vol. 5, Ch. 3, Equations 3.1–3.3. The model spreads methane over years as the waste decays. We count the total it will ever emit, once, on the day it is thrown away.

    Q8 · Production factors and category mapping
    
  
    Values come from OWID: GHG emissions per kg of food product (Poore & Nemecek 2018). The CSV was fetched on 2026-11-21 and has one data year, 2010. They cover the supply chain from farm to retail: land use, farming, feed, processing, transport, packaging and retail. Disposal isn't included, so the landfill factor doesn't double-count it. Every value is per kg of raw product.
    
  
    
  
    Cooked-weight correction (optional)
    
  
    Waste is weighed cooked, but OWID values are per kg raw. "Cooked kg per raw kg" divides the production factor. For example, 1 kg of raw rice cooks to about 2.5 kg, so 1 kg of cooked rice holds only 0.4 kg of raw rice. Leave it at 1 for no correction. Only rice has a suggested value (from the handoff). Any other number you enter is your own estimate. Meat often loses weight when cooked, so its value is below 1.

    Source notes, verified in this session
    
  
      
    
    • DOCf: the handoff proposed 0.5. The 2019 Refinement, Table 3.0 (New), p. 3.12, recommends 0.7 for "highly decomposable wastes, e.g. food wastes". 0.5 is the default for mixed bulk waste of unknown composition.
      • 
    
    • MCF: Table 3.1 (Updated), p. 3.13. 0.6 = uncategorised, 1.0 = managed anaerobic, 0.8 = unmanaged deep (≥5 m), 0.4 = unmanaged shallow, plus the semi-aerobic and active-aeration options.
      • 
    
    • F = 0.5: p. 3.14, "the use of the IPCC default value … (0.5) is therefore encouraged."
      • 
    
    • OX: Table 3.2, p. 3.14. 0 is the default. 0.1 applies only to managed sites covered with methane-oxidising material such as soil or compost.
      • 
    
    • Equations: 3.2 gives decomposable carbon (W·DOC·DOCf·MCF). 3.3 and 3.6 multiply by F·16/12 to get methane. 3.1 subtracts recovery and oxidation, (1−OX). We assume recovery R = 0.
      • 
    
    • Not verified in this session: DOC = 0.15 for food waste comes from the 2006 Guidelines, Vol. 5, Ch. 2, Table 2.4, which is not in the Ch. 3 PDF. The issue fixes the value. GWP₁₀₀ = 27.0 for non-fossil CH₄ (AR6 WG1 Table 7.15) comes from memory. The other options are AR6 fossil CH₄ = 29.8 and AR5 = 28. Check both before seeding.
      • 
    
    • Biogenic CO₂ from rotting food isn't counted, as in IPCC inventories. Only the methane is counted.
